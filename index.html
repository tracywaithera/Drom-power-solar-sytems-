Solar Engineering Knowledge & FAQ Hub

Technical Guidance for Kenyan & East African Solar

Verified engineering benchmarks from Drom Power Systems Ltd’s Nairobi technical team.

How much do solar water heaters save in Nairobi and across Kenya?

Drom Power evacuated tube and flat plate solar water heaters reduce total household and institutional electricity bills by up to 65%. They supply 90% of hot water requirements during sunny months and up to 40% during overcast weather, with a single flat plate collector heating 200 to 400+ litres per day across a 25+ year lifespan.

LiFePO4 vs. Gel batteries for Kenyan power backup: Which is better?

Drom Power LiFePO4 batteries (12.8V 100Ah–300Ah and 24V/48V wall/rack banks) deliver 2,000 to 5,000+ cycles (>80% capacity retention), double the capacity-to-volume ratio of lead-acid, zero memory effect, and an integrated Smart BMS. Drom Power Gel & Deep Cycle batteries (such as Model 6GFM120 12V 120Ah) offer rugged, maintenance-free, non-spillable backup at a lower initial CAPEX.

How are off-grid solar water pumps sized in East Africa?

Drom Power sizes PV arrays at just 10% higher wattage than the rated pump motor. Options include Direct DC Brush pumps (no controller needed, 100% copper winding), DC Brushless permanent-magnet pumps, and AC-Inverter Submersible pumps—all featuring automatic dry-well and full-tank level protection.

Why do Kenyan boarding schools choose SUS 304 electric & solar cooking pots?

CE-certified SUS 304 stainless steel institutional boilers eliminate open flames, LPG cylinder explosion risks, and firewood costs. Equipped with a one-click water inlet faucet, bottom drainage valve, and digital temperature control, they integrate seamlessly with Drom Power commercial solar arrays.
